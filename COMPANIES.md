## How to read a job description

Copy the description into a table with four columns: **requirement**, **evidence I have**, **gap**, and **next action**. Separate must-have skills from nice-to-have tools. If the description says “experience with Python,” the evidence should be a maintained project with tests and decisions—not merely a course certificate.

Look for recurring nouns and verbs. Nouns reveal the domain and stack; verbs reveal the work: define, measure, experiment, model, deploy, monitor, influence, or communicate. Match your resume and portfolio bullets to outcomes and decisions rather than listing every library.

## Portfolio-to-role mapping

| If the target role emphasizes… | Make sure your portfolio shows… |
|---|---|
| Experimentation | A clear estimand, randomization or identification assumptions, power, guardrails, and decision |
| Business impact | A stakeholder memo, metric definition, trade-off, and measurable recommendation |
| Production ML | Tests, API/batch interface, versioning, monitoring, rollback, and cost/latency discussion |
| Deep modeling | Strong baseline, ablations, reproducible experiments, error taxonomy, and literature context |
| Analytics | Reliable SQL, metric layer, data validation, dashboard or memo, and stakeholder-friendly language |

## Ethical job-search practice

Do not copy proprietary interview questions, confidential datasets, or internal code into a public repository. Use public or synthetic data, cite original sources, remove credentials and personal information, and describe your work honestly. A polished repository should demonstrate judgment as well as technical ability.
